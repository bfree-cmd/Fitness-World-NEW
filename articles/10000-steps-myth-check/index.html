What research shows

In a large cohort of middle-aged adults, taking roughly 7,000 or more steps per day was associated with substantially lower mortality than taking fewer than 7,000. Other studies also suggest benefits rise across a range of step counts.

What matters more than the magic number

Moving more than your current baseline is useful. Someone averaging 3,000 steps may benefit from working toward 5,000 or 6,000 rather than treating 10,000 as pass-or-fail.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Do you need exactly 10,000 steps a day?

No. Health benefits can appear below 10,000 steps, and the number is better treated as a practical target than a biological threshold.

Are fewer daily steps still useful?

Yes. Increasing activity from a low baseline can be meaningful even when you do not reach 10,000 steps.

Should everyone use the same step goal?

No. A useful goal depends on baseline activity, ability, health and what is realistic enough to repeat.
