The short answer

There is promising evidence that some ashwagandha preparations can reduce perceived stress and anxiety compared with placebo. That does not mean every product, dose or person will get the same result.

What the research found

A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis included nine randomized trials with 558 participants. Pooled results favored ashwagandha for perceived stress, anxiety scores and cortisol, while several studies reported mild to moderate adverse events.

Why product details matter

Ashwagandha is not one standardized product. Extracts, concentrations and doses vary, so results from one formulation cannot automatically be transferred to every supplement on a store shelf.

What remains uncertain

Many trials are relatively small and short. The review authors noted that more information is needed about long-term safety.

Practical takeaway

Ashwagandha has a better evidence base for stress than many wellness supplements, but it is not a universal stress treatment. Product quality, medication interactions and individual health context matter.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Does ashwagandha reduce stress?

Some randomized trials and recent reviews report reductions in perceived stress or anxiety with specific ashwagandha preparations, but the evidence is not universal.

Are all ashwagandha supplements equivalent?

No. Extract type, dose and standardization vary, so results from one preparation cannot automatically be applied to every product.

Is ashwagandha right for everyone?

No. Safety, medications, pregnancy, thyroid issues and individual health context can matter, so supplement decisions should not be based on a trend alone.

EVIDENCE CONTEXT

Promising stress data, with real uncertainty

Meta-analyses of randomized trials report improvements in some stress and anxiety outcomes, but certainty and consistency remain limitations. A newer review found a cortisol reduction without a clear improvement in perceived stress, while other pooled analyses report benefits on both. Fitness World therefore treats the signal as promising rather than settled, especially for long-term use and broad clinical claims.
