## Findings from the previous report that should not be repeated

- **References already have external hyperlinks.** PDF annotations on pages 77–78 contain NIH, PubMed, eCFR, MedlinePlus and Extension URLs. They are not link-free titles.
- **The jump from [19] to [33]/[34] is explained.** Page 77 explicitly identifies these as later food-safety references and says numbering is intentionally non-sequential. Renumbering is optional editorial cleanup.
- **Authorship is not wholly absent.** Fitness World appears throughout and in Author/Creator metadata. What is missing is an explicit edition and truthful human/AI/medical-review statement.
- **Omega-3 atrial-fibrillation risk is already disclosed on page 72.** Keep that caution. [NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Omega3FattyAcids-HealthProfessional/) describes the high-dose trial context.
- **The nutrient ratings already say “essential role,” not unconditional strong supplement benefit.** Improve their clarity rather than claiming the body text makes a universal benefit promise.
- **The cover does not guarantee a medical outcome.** The book explicitly excludes guaranteed results, detox and physiological “reset” claims. A more cautious cover line is optional.

## Keep these existing strengths

Keep the distinction between food/tea and concentrated products, the no-guaranteed-results statement, the non-diagnostic habit score, gradual fiber advice, allergy/medicine cautions, the as-needed reference-library structure, and the warning about improvised room-temperature garlic/herb oils. These are useful safeguards.

## Limits

The proposed numerical additions above were checked against current NIH/MedlinePlus guidance. This audit did not independently verify every recipe, every reference, all external links or every clinical claim. The chia abstract remained inaccessible, and no current exhaustive rosemary/cocoa evidence search was completed. A qualified reviewer should check any revised supplement or safety instructions before a new paid edition is released.
